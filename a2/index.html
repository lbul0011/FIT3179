Public transport in Victoria

How do Victorians travel?

There are primarily 3 types of public transport available in Victoria: Buses, trains and trams. There are metropolitan and regional train services, in addition to metropolitan and regional buses. Victoria is home to the largest tram network in the world, it serves the inner-city region of Melbourne.

Prior to the COVID-19 pandemic, a total of around 180 million public transport trips were taken. Post-pandemic public transport usage has yet to recover to these levels. Since reaching a low of 68.48 million trips in 2020, each subsequent year has shown a steady increase in usage.

Unsuprisingly, the most popular transport options are the metropolitan services - trains, buses and trams. Trains take top spot, followed by trams and then buses. Click each of the entries on the legend to emphasise particular transport modes.

The monthly average patronage for Victoria's various public transport modes paints an interesting picture. The periodic school holidays are clearly visible. The end/start of each is characterised by drops in normal patronage. Also visible are the average weekend passenger trends.

Public transport punctuality

The punctuality of Victoria's various public transport options paints an interesting story. The effects of the COVID-19 pandemic are extremely clear, with the on-time punctuality of all services jumping quite dramatically. This is the result of a reduced number of services running during this period, easing pressure on the transit system considerably.

On average (as indicated by the dashed lines) it's clear that metropolitan trains are the most punctual. Buses - suprisingly - are the second most punctual option, despite having to contend with road traffic. Trams are the most likely to be late, perhaps owing to their shared use of roads in the congested CBD.

Unfortunately for tram users, on-time performance is currently about the same after the pandemic as it was before. All other transport options have improved their punctuality after the pandemic and retained this increase. As shown above, this may be the direct result of less people using services.

A deeper dive on Melbourne's metropolitan trains

The Victorian train network is incredibly expansive. Combined, there are a total of 222 passenger train stations in Victoria. Here you can see the busiest and least busiest stations in the network. It's clear that suburban train stations are extensively used by Melbournians. By passenger count, the CBD stations are the busiest - reflecting the concentrated workforce in the city and the limited alternative transit options (see above map for further details).

As urban sprawl continues across Melbourne, there is a shifting trend in the demand placed on outer suburban stations. You can see this in the outer Western and Northern lines, in stations such as Werribee (1,049,600), Watergardens (1,295,450) and Craigieburne (1,508,250). These stations are responsible for serving some of the fastest growing pockets of Melbourne, and reflect the increasing pressures placed on the public train system.

Train usage trends

The Victorian train schedule can be broadly categorised into the following groups:

  • Pre AM: 12:00am - 6:59am
  • AM Peak: 7:00am - 9:29am
  • Interpeak: 9:30am - 2:59pm
  • PM Peak: 3:00pm - 6:59pm
  • PM Late: 7:00pm - 11:59pm

In general, the busiest times for the train system are during the AM peak. This reflects the high demand for the system both by students and those commuting to work. There is also considerable demand on the system in the PM peak and interpeak periods. Interestingly, during 2020 (when Victoria was largely shutdown due to the COVID-19 pandemic) the highest peak periods shifted from the AM peak to the PM peak.

Use the year dropdown to explore further trends in patronage from 2018-2022. Note the heatmap scale is not fixed, it shows the relative patronage for that year and shouldn't be used to compare years against each other.

Certain train lines are more heavily used by school aged passengers. Here the difference between the average number of passengers on school holidays vs. normal weekdays is shown. All lines (except Flemington Racecourse) exhibit a decrease in patronage during school holidays, with the Pakenham and Hughesdale lines showing the largest drops. The increase in patronage on the Flemington Racecourse line during school holidays is likely the result of the various events hosted at the Melbourne Showgrounds and Flemington Racecourse.

Demand across the metropolitan train network has remained fairly steady across the networks various lines. As with other forms of transport the most drastic changes to this behaviour occured during the COVID-19 pandemic. Interestingly, the rapid growth of Melbourne's outer suburbs is reflected in the increased demand on lines serving these areas such as the Pakenham and Cranbourne lines.
